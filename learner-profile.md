## Known weak spots / recurring errors
- Doubles consonant in "Guten" → writes "Gutten"
- Lowercase formal Sie/Ihnen — needs to remember always capitalized (seen with both Ihnen and Sie, incl. in imperatives like "Gehen sie" → "Gehen Sie", L14)
- Word order with "auch" — was recurring (L1–L6), but drilled in review session and got 4/4 correct — may be solidifying
- "Es geht's" (wrong) vs "Es geht" (correct) in answers
- Adjective endings: -e vs -es (e.g. *eine schöne Stadt* not *eine schönes Stadt*)
- Forgets to capitalize nouns mid-sentence (Stadt, Nachname, Lieblingsstadt)
- Mixes English/German words: "English" instead of "Englisch", "is" instead of "ist" (persistent across L3–L5)
- Capitalizes "Ich" mid-sentence (recurring across multiple lessons — needs drilling)
- Masculine nouns after haben: uses "ein/kein" instead of "einen/keinen" (first seen L5) — drilled in review session, showed clear improvement
- *kein/keine* for plurals still slipping (wrote "kein Kinder" → should be "keine Kinder")
- Irregular plurals: drops umlaut (Kinde → Kinder, Sohne → Söhne) — needs drilling
- leben vs lieben confusion (one letter difference — to live vs to love)
- *möchte* vs *möchten*: applies regular -t ending to er (*möchtet*) — modal verbs don't do this; ich = er = möchte
- English words still sneaking in: "beer" instead of "Bier" (same pattern as "is"/"English" in earlier lessons)
- *einen Kaffee* (masc. acc.) keeps slipping — reverts to *ein Kaffee* even after correction in L7
- Umlaut plurals: forgets umlaut on Supermarkt→Supermärkte, Stadt→Städte
- Verb agreement with collective singular: wrote *wohnen Ihre Familie* → should be *wohnt* (Familie is singular)
- *schön* vs *schon*: wrote *schon* (already) instead of *schön* (nice) in a set phrase
- Capitalizes lowercase nouns used mid-sentence beyond obvious ones: *essen*→*Essen*, *zeit*→*Zeit* (same root pattern as "forgets to capitalize nouns" — extends to less-obvious nouns like verb/noun pairs, e.g. essen (to eat) vs Essen (food))
- Stacks two fronted phrases in position 1 (*Am liebsten am Wochenende spiele ich...* ✗) — only one phrase may occupy position 1; needs reminding to pick one
- Gender articles slip under pressure in free production even for known nouns (*die Supermarkt* → *der Supermarkt*, L14) — distinct from not knowing the gender; happens when juggling other new grammar (imperative) at the same time
- müssen conjugation: drops the -st for du (*du muss* → *du musst*, L14) — same family as earlier möchte/möchtet mix-up but on a different modal
- Occasionally over-capitalizes verbs mid-sentence (*Nimm* → *nimm*, L14) — likely spillover from noun-capitalization habit; opposite-direction version of the usual error, worth a light note if it recurs
- Plural nouns that look singular: treats *Schmerzen* (always plural) as masculine singular → *der Zahnschmerzen* instead of *die Zahnschmerzen* (L15) — same family as *Kinder*/umlaut-plural slips, but this time the trap is the noun's plural-only form not being obviously plural
- v/w spelling confusion: *vidersehen* instead of *Wiedersehen* (L15) — new, watch if it recurs
- Wrong preposition/near-homophone under pressure: *über* instead of *aber* (L15) — likely reaching for a similar-sounding word rather than a true grammar gap
- Kaffee spelling (missing double e, "Kaffe") resurfaced in L16 — same slip first seen L7, still not fully solid
- Verb conjugation drifts to infinitive/plural form in coordinated clauses when subject is omitted: "...und haben Obst gekauft" instead of "...und habe..." (L16) — new pattern, watch if it recurs
- New verb-helper mix-up: assumes any verb about an activity done "away from home" takes sein — used *bin geschleft* for schlafen (L16); schlafen takes haben since it's not movement, just happens in place
